Our goal in this section is to prove the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}{Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions}{eq_and_un}
    Let $u_0, u_{b, 0} \in \Ltwo{\partial \Omega}$ and $v_0 \in \Ltwo{\Omega}$ be essentially bounded and nonnegative initial conditions. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ and $v \in \pdespace$ to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init}. Moreover, these solutions are also nonnegative.
\end{theorem}

As previously mentioned, the proof will be based on an application of the Banach fixed-point theorem. If you have followed a course on ODE theory, chances are that you have seen the Picard-Lindel\"of Theorem and its proof. The proof for our model follows the same general idea, but requires some extra steps to handle our situation of a coupled system. We ``freeze'' the terms in the ODEs that depend on $v$ so we can use the standard existence and uniqueness properties we know about such systems to get solutions $u$ and $u_b$ to this alternative problem. The PDE gets a similar treatment, producing solutions $v$ of some alternative PDE, after which we can ``update'' the frozen terms in the ODE. This produces an operator $\mathcal{T}$ that maps any $v \in \pdespace$ to another $\widetilde{v} \in \pdespace$. If we can then show that this operator is a contraction, the Banach fixed-point theorem will guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a fixed point $v^* \in \pdespace$, which will be our solution to the PDE.

Let us first concretely demonstrate what we mean by ``freezing'' the terms that depend on $v$. Fix $v^* \in \pdespace$ and consider the modified system of ODEs given by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen_odes}
    \begin{aligned}
        u_t         &= - f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*) + p(u)    & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma, \\
        u_{b,t}     &= f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*) + q(u_b)    & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma. \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent
These ODEs are now \emph{decoupled} from the PDE, so we can freely talk about the existence and uniqueness of solutions to this system without simultaneously having to worry about $v$ satisfying the PDE.

\begin{lemma}{Existence and uniqueness for the decoupled ODEs}{exun_frozen_odes}
    Let $u_0, u_{b,0} \in \Ltwo{\Gamma}$ be essentially bounded and nonnegative initial conditions and fix $v^* \in \pdespace$. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ to \cref{eq:frozen_odes} satisfying these initial conditions. Moreover, these solutions are also nonnegative and bounded by a constant depending on $T$.
\end{lemma}

Using these two lemmas, we can define two operators between $\pdespace$ and $\odespace^2$. One takes two arbitrary functions $(u^*, u_b^*) \in \odespace^2$ and maps it to the unique solution $v \in \pdespace$ to \cref{eq:frozen_pde}. Let us refer to this operator as $\mathcal{T}_{1}: \odespace^2 \to \pdespace$. The other, $\mathcal{T}_{2}: \pdespace \to \odespace^2$, takes a function $v^* \in \pdespace$ and defines $\mathcal{T}_{2}(v^*)$ as the unique solution $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$ of \cref{eq:frozen_odes}. Combining these two operators, we define

\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{T} := \mathcal{T}_{2} \circ \mathcal{T}_{1} : \odespace^2 \to \odespace^2.
\end{equation*}

\noindent
We consider what it means for this operator to have a fixed point. That is, suppose we have functions $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$ such that $\mathcal{T}(u, u_b) = (u, u_b)$, and let $v \in \pdespace$ denote the intermediate value $v = \mathcal{T}_{1}(u, u_b)$. Given that then $\mathcal{T}_{2}(v) = (u, u_b)$, by definition this means that $v$ solves \cref{eq:weak_pde} for almost every $t \in (0, T)$, and $w = (u, u_b)$ solves \cref{eq:weak_ode} for almost every $t \in (0, T)$ and $x \in \Gamma$, meaning that $v$, $u$ and $u_b$ are weak solutions of our model. Conversely, if $v$, $u$ and $u_b$ are some weak solutions to the model, then by the uniqueness properties of \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes,lem:exun_frozen_pde}, we must have that $\mathcal{T}_{2}(v) = (u, u_b)$ and $\mathcal{T}_{1}(u, u_b) = v$, and thus $\mathcal{T}(u, u_b) = (u, u_b)$.

It should be clear now why we are looking to apply the Banach fixed point theorem. If we prove that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction, we can guarantee the existence of a fixed point and therefore a weak solution. Since every weak solution of the model is also a fixed point, the theorem also grants us uniqueness of such solutions. To prove that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction, we first show this for $\mathcal{T}_{2}$ and $\mathcal{T}_{1}$ separately, after which we can easily combine the results.

    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{1}{2} &\norm{v(t)}_{\Ltwo{\Omega}}^2 + (\delta - C_\gamma C_\varepsilon \varepsilon) \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}}^2 \leq B_\varepsilon(T) \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \koff A_{\varepsilon} \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 .
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    for almost all $t$. If we now choose $\varepsilon < \frac{\delta}{C_\gamma C_\varepsilon}$, we can estimate both the $\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}$-norm and the $\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Omega}}$-norm of $v$. The first will only be helpful later, but the latter concludes this proof. First, since $\delta - C_\gamma C_\varepsilon \varepsilon > 0$, we have

    \begin{equation}\label{eq:extra_estimate}
    \begin{aligned}
        \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}}^2 \leq D_\varepsilon(T) \left(\norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2\right) .
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}

    \noindent
    Second, we can integrate from 0 to $T$ once more to get

    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Omega}}}^2 \leq T M_\varepsilon(T) \left(\norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2\right).
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    If we trace back the definitions of this constant $M_\varepsilon(T)$, we see that it is continuous in $T$ with a finite value at $T = 0$. This means that for small enough $T$, we will have that $T M_\varepsilon(T) < 1$, finishing this proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}{}{contraction_odes}
    For $T > 0$ small enough, $\mathcal{T}_{2}: \pdespace \to \odespace^2$ is a contraction. That is, there is a $q \in [0, 1)$ such that
    
    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{\mathcal{T}_{2} (\vb) - \mathcal{T}_{2} (\vt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 \leq q \norm{\vb - \vt}_\pdespace^2
    \end{equation*}

    for every $\vb, \vt \in \pdespace$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Now let $\vb, \vt \in \pdespace$ and define $\ub, \ubb, \ut$ and $\ubt$ by $(\ub, \ubb) := \mathcal{T}_{2}(\vb)$ and $(\ut, \ubt) := \mathcal{T}_{2}(\vt)$. Once again, we denote their differences by

    \begin{equation*}
        v := \vb - \vt, \qquad u := \ub - \ut \qquad \text{and} \qquad u_b := \ubb - \ubt,
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    such that

    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{\mathcal{T}_{2} (\vb) - \mathcal{T}_{2} (\vt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 = \norm{(\ub, \ubb) - (\ut, \ubt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 = \norm{(u, u_b)}_{\odespace^2}^2 = \norm{u}_{\odespace}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\odespace}^2.
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    We therefore seek to estimate these separate differences $u$ and $u_b$ in terms of $v$. Starting off with $u$, we see that it satisfies
    \todo{first step?}
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\d}{\d t} \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 &= 2 \int_\Gamma u_t u \d S \\
        &= 2 \int_\Gamma (\ub_t - \ut_t) u \d S \\
        &= 2 \int_\Gamma \left( \alpha (\ubb - \ubt) - \delta_u (\ub - \ut) - 2 \kon (\ub^2 \vb - \ut^2 \vt) + 2 \koff (\ubb - \ubt) \right) u \d S \\
        &= 2 (\alpha + 2 \koff) \int_\Gamma u u_b \d S - 2 \delta_u \int_\Gamma u^2 \d S - 4 \kon \int_\Gamma (\ub^2 \vb - \ut^2 \vt) u \d S
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    for almost every $t$. As we did for the previous lemma, we now use the identity from \cref{eq:contraction_identity_1} to further derive

    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\d}{\d t} \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 &= 2 (\alpha + 2 \koff) \int_\Gamma u u_b \d S - 2 \delta_u \int_\Gamma u^2 \d S - \overbrace{4 \kon \int_\Gamma (\ub + \ut) \vb u^2 \d S }^{\geq 0} - 4 \kon \int_\Gamma \ut^2 v u \d S \\
        &\leq 2 (\alpha + 2 \koff) \int_\Gamma u u_b \d S - 2 \delta_u \int_\Gamma u^2 \d S + 4 \kon \int_\Gamma \ut^2 \left| v u \right| \d S \\
        &\leq 2(\alpha + 2 \koff) \int_\Gamma u u_b \d S - 2 \delta_u \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + 4 \kon M_u (T)^2 \int_\Gamma | v u | \d S . \\
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}
    
    \noindent
    To further separate the mixed terms, we use Young's inequality twice and estimate

    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\d}{\d t} \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 &\leq A_1(T) \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + A_2 \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + \widetilde{B_1}(T) \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 , \\
        &\leq A_1(T) \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + A_2 \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + B_1(T) \norm{v}_{H^1(\Omega)}^2 , \\
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    where $A_1(T)$, $A_2$, $\widetilde{B_1}(T)$ and $B_1(T)$ are defined as

    \begin{equation*}
        A_1(T) := - 2 \delta_u + \alpha + 2 \koff + 2 \kon M_u (T)^2 , \qquad A_2 := \alpha + 2 \koff
    \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*}
        \widetilde{B_1}(T) := 2 \kon M_u (T)^2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad B_1(T) := C_\gamma \widetilde{B_1}(T) .
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    Using the same arguments, we get a similar estimate for $\frac{\d}{\d t} \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2$, namely

    \begingroup
    \allowdisplaybreaks
    \begin{align*}
        \frac{\d}{\d t} \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 &= 2 \int_\Gamma \left(\left(\ubb\right)_t - \left(\ubt\right)_t\right) u_b \d S \\
        &= - 2 \left(\delta_{u_b} + \koff\right) \int_\Gamma u_b^2 \d S + 2 \kon \int_\Gamma \left(\ub^2 \vb - \ut^2 \vt\right) u_b \d S \\
        &= - 2 \left(\delta_{u_b} + \koff\right) \int_\Gamma u_b^2 \d S + 2 \kon \int_\Gamma \left(\ub + \ut\right) \vt u u_b \d S + 2 \kon \int_\Gamma \ut^2 v u_b \d S \\
        &\leq - 2 \left(\delta_{u_b} + \koff\right) \int_\Gamma u_b^2 \d S + 2 \kon \int_\Gamma \left(\ub + \ut\right) \vt \left| u u_b \right| \d S + 2 \kon \int_\Gamma \ut^2 \left| v u_b \right| \d S \\
        &\leq - 2 \left(\delta_{u_b} + \koff\right) \int_\Gamma u_b^2 \d S + 4 \kon M_u(T) M_v(T) \int_\Gamma \left| u u_b \right| \d S + 2 \kon M_u(T)^2 \int_\Gamma \left| v u_b \right| \d S \\
        &\leq A_3(T) \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + A_4(T) \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + \widetilde{B_2}(T) \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 ,\\
        &\leq A_3(T) \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + A_4(T) \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + B_2(T) \norm{v}_{H^1(\Omega)}^2 ,\\
    \end{align*}
    \endgroup

    \noindent
    where $A_3(T)$, $A_4(T)$, $\widetilde{B_2}(T)$ and $B_2(T)$ are given by

    \begin{equation*}
        A_3(T) := 2 \kon M_u(T) M_v(T) + \kon M_u(T)^2 - 2 \delta_{u_b} - 2 \koff ,
    \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*}
        A_4(T) := 2 \kon M_u(T) M_v(T), \qquad \widetilde{B_2}(T) := \kon M_u(T)^2  \qquad \text{and} \qquad B_2(T) := C_\gamma \widetilde{B_2}(T) .
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    To merge these estimates, we let $A(T) := 2 \max \left(A_1(T), A_2, A_3(T), A_4(T)\right)$ and $B(T) := 2 \max \left(B_1(T), B_2(T)\right)$ and sum to get \todo{overflow}

    \begin{equation*}
        \frac{\d}{\d t} \left(\norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2\right) \leq A(T) \left(\norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2\right) + B(T) \norm{v}_{H^1(\Omega)}^2
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    for almost all $t$. An application of Gronwall's inequality gives

    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \norm{u(t)}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + \norm{u_b(t)}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 &\leq \norm{u(0)}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + \norm{u_b(0)}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + B(T) \int_{0}^{t} \norm{v(s)}_{H^1(\Omega)}^2 e^{(t - s) A(T)} \d s \\
        &\leq B(T) e^{T A(T)} \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}}^2 , \\
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    since the initial conditions of $\ub$ and $\ut$ are the same, and therefore $u(0) = 0$ for every $x \in \Gamma$. Naturally, the same holds for $u_b(0)$. After integrating from 0 to $T$, we can conclude that

    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 \leq T B(T) e^{T A(T)} \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}}^2 .
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    It may seem like we are done now, but the current estimate uses the wrong norm on $v$. To get an estimate using the $\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Omega}}$-norm instead, we can use \cref{eq:extra_estimate} to get

    \begin{multline}
        \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 \\
        \leq T B(T) e^{T A(T)} D_\varepsilon(T) \left(\norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2\right) + \overbrace{T B(T) e^{T A(T)} \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Omega}}}^2}^{\geq 0},
    \end{multline}

    \noindent
    or after rearranging terms,

    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 \leq \frac{T B(T) e^{T A(T)}}{1 - T B(T) e^{T A(T)} D_\varepsilon(T)} \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Omega}}}^2.
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    As this factor clearly approaches 0 when $T$ goes to 0, we can lower $T$ until it is less than 1 and conclude the proof.
\end{proof}

We have now gathered enough results to prove the main statement of this section.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:eq_and_un}]
    Let $\widetilde{T} > 0$ be small enough such that, by \cref{lem:contraction_pde,lem:contraction_odes}, $\mathcal{T}_1$ and $\mathcal{T}_2$ are contractions with Lipschitz constants $q_1$ and $q_2$, respectively. Define, as discussed before, $\mathcal{T}$ as the composition of $\mathcal{T}_1$ and $\mathcal{T}_2$. Then for any $(\ub, \ubb), (\ut, \ubt) \in \odespace^2$, we have

    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{\mathcal{T}(\ub, \ubb) - \mathcal{T}(\ut, \ubt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 \leq q_2 \norm{\mathcal{T}_1(\ub, \ubb) - \mathcal{T}_1(\ut, \ubt)}_{\pdespace}^2 \leq q_2 q_1 \norm{(\ub, \ubb) - (\ut, \ubt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 ,
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    showing that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction since $q_2 q_1 \in [0, 1)$.

    We apply the Banach fixed point theorem to get a unique fixed point $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$.
\end{proof}
