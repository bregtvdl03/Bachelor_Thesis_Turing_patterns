Our goal in this section is to prove the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}{Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions}{eq_and_un}
    Let $u_0, u_{b, 0} \in L^2(\partial \Omega)$ and $v_0 \in L^2(\Omega)$ be non-negative initial conditions. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ and $v \in \pdespace$ to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init}. Moreover, these solutions are also non-negative.
\end{theorem}

As previously mentioned, the proof will be based on an application of the Banach fixed-point theorem. If you have followed a course on ODE theory, chances are that you have seen the Picard-Lindel\"of Theorem and its proof. The proof for our model follows the same general idea, but requires some extra steps to handle our situation of a coupled system. We ``freeze'' the terms in the ODEs that depend on $v$ so we can use the standard existence and uniqueness properties we know about such systems to get solutions $u$ and $u_b$ to this alternative problem. The PDE gets a similar treatment, producing solutions $v$ of some alternative PDE, after which we can ``update'' the frozen terms in the ODE. This produces an operator $\mathcal{T}$ that maps any $v \in \pdespace$ to another $\widetilde{v} \in \pdespace$. If we can then show that this operator is a contraction, the Banach fixed-point theorem will guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a fixed point $v^* \in \pdespace$, which will be our solution to the PDE.

Let us first concretely demonstrate what we mean by ``freezing'' the terms that depend on $v$. Fix $v^* \in \pdespace$ and consider the modified system of ODEs given by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen_odes}
    \begin{aligned}
        u_t         &= - f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*) + p(u)    & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma, \\
        u_{b,t}     &= f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*) + q(u_b)    & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma, \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent
where $\gamma v^*$ denotes the trace of $v^*$ as discussed in \cref{sec:weak_derivatives}. These ODEs are now \emph{decoupled} from the PDE, so we can freely talk about the existence and uniqueness of solutions to this system, without simultaneously having to worry about $v$ satisfying the PDE.

\begin{lemma}{Existence and uniqueness for the decoupled ODEs}{exun_frozen_odes}
    Let $u_0, u_{b,0} \in L^2(\partial \Omega)$ be non-negative initial conditions and fix $v^* \in \pdespace$. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ to \cref{eq:frozen_odes} satisfying these initial conditions. Moreover, these solutions are also non-negative and bounded by a constant depending on $T$.
\end{lemma}

    Fix $x$ and define $w$ and $F$ as in \cref{eq:weak_ode,eq:ode_system_function}, except using $\gamma v^*$ instead of $v$. We will first show that $F$ satisfies the requirements for \cref{thm:caratheodory}. We can then apply this theorem to get a local solution, and prove that we can extend it to the entirety $[0, T]$. We will see that this solution satisfies the non-negativity and boundedness requirement, that the solution $w$ is unique, and that its components $u$ and $u_b$ have the desired regularity in both space and time.

    Set $t_0 = 0$ and $x_0 = (u_0(x), u_{b, 0}(x))$. For every fixed $t$, $F$ is continuous in $w$, and since $\gamma v^*$ is measurable in time and $F$ depends continuously on $\gamma v^*$, $F$ is measurable in $t$ for every fixed $w$. The continuity of $F$ in $w$ also implies that $F$ is bounded in $w$ in any rectangle $R$ we choose, so we only need $\gamma v^*$ to be of sufficient time-regularity such that we have a time-dependent measurable function $m$ that bounds $|F|$ in $R$. \todo{time-regularity of $\gamma v^*$}

        w_2(t) := \begin{cases}
            \widetilde{w}_1(t) & \text{ if } t \in [0, \beta_1), \\
            \widetilde{w}_2(t) & \text{ if } t \in [\beta_1, \beta_1 + \beta_2], \\
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    for $t \in [0, \beta_1 + \beta_2]$. We can keep defining new extensions iteratively. At iteration $i$, we have a definition of $w_i$ on $[0, \beta_1 + \dots + \beta_i]$, so we set
    
    \begin{equation*}

    \noindent
    and apply \cref{thm:caratheodory} to get a new local extension $\widetilde{w}_{i + 1}$ on the interval $|t - t_0| < \beta_{i + 1}$ for some $\beta_{i + 1} > 0$. This allows us to define a new function $w_{i + 1}$ by

    \begin{equation*}
        w_{i + 1}(t) := \begin{cases}
            \widetilde{w}_1(t) & \text{ if } t \in [0, \beta_1), \\
            \widetilde{w}_2(t) & \text{ if } t \in [\beta_1, \beta_1 + \beta_2), \\
            \quad\vdots \\
            \widetilde{w}_{i + 1}(t) & \text{ if } t \in [\beta_1 + \dots + \beta_i, \beta_1 + \dots + \beta_{i + 1}], \\

Since $\left( T_i \right)_{i \geq 1}$ is strictly increasing, it either diverges to infinity, converges to a value greater than or equal to $T$, or it converges to a value $\widetilde{T} \in (0, T)$. Using \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes}, we define an operator $\mathcal{T}_1: \pdespace \to \odespace^2$ that maps any function $v^* \in \pdespace$ to a tuple $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$ of solutions to \cref{eq:frozen_odes}.

We can use a similar method for the PDE in \cref{sys:PDEs}. Fix $(u^*, u_b^*) \in \odespace^2$ and consider the modified weak formulation given by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen_pde}
    \begin{aligned}
        \inner{v_t, \phi}_{L^2(\Omega)} &= - \inner{f(u^*, u_b^*, v), \phi}_{L^2(\Gamma)} + \inner{r(v), \phi}_{L^2(\Gamma)} - D \inner{\nabla v, \nabla \phi}_{L^2(\Omega; \R^d)} \\
        &= \inner{g(v), \phi}_{L^2(\Gamma)} - D \inner{\nabla v, \nabla \phi}_{L^2(\Omega; \R^d)}, \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent
where $g(v) := - f(u^*, u_b^*, v) + r(v)$. Note that we can only make this definition because $u^*$ and $u_b^*$ are fixed. We have now decoupled the PDE from the ODEs, and we can prove a result that is nearly identical to \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes}.

Then there exists a unique weak solution $v \in \pdespace$ to \cref{eq:frozen_pde} satisfying these initial conditions. Moreover, this solution is also non-negative and bounded by a constant depending on $T$.
\end{lemma}

This result allows us to define another operator $\mathcal{T}_2: \odespace^2 \to \pdespace$ sending a tuple $(u^*, u_b^*) \in \odespace^2$ to a solution $v \in \pdespace$ of \cref{eq:frozen_pde}. By combining this step with the previous one, we can use $\mathcal{T} := \mathcal{T}_2 \circ \mathcal{T}_1: \pdespace \to \pdespace$ to iteratively get new solutions. At each step, we modify \cref{eq:frozen_odes} using the input from $\pdespace$ to get a new pair $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$, with which we proceed to update \cref{eq:frozen_pde} to get a new $v \in \pdespace$.

\begin{lemma}{}{contraction}
    For $\widetilde{T} > 0$ small enough, $\mathcal{T}: \pdespace \to \pdespace$ is a contraction. That is, there is a $q \in [0, 1)$ such that
    
    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{\mathcal{T} v}_\pdespace \leq q \norm{v}_\pdespace
    \end{equation*}

    for every $v \in \pdespace$.
\end{lemma}

We have now gathered enough results to prove the main statement of this section.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:eq_and_un}]
    Using \cref{lem:contraction}, let $T > 0$ such that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction. Then by the Banach fixed-point theorem, there exists a unique fixed point $v^*$ of $\mathcal{T}$. Next, define $(u^*, u_b^*) := \mathcal{T}_1 v^*$. Then by definition, $u^*$ and $u_b^*$ solve \cref{eq:frozen_odes} with $v^*$ as the frozen input. On the other hand, $\mathcal{T}_2 (u^*, u_b^*) = \mathcal{T}v^* = v^*$ solves \cref{eq:frozen_pde} with $u^*$ and $u_b^*$ as the inputs to the frozen reactions. In other words, $u^*$, $u_b^*$ and $v^*$ are weak solutions to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init}.

    To show uniqueness of the weak solutions, it is enough to note that for any weak solution $u^*, u_b^*$ and $v^*$, we have that $v^*$ is a fixed point of $\mathcal{T}$. This follows directly from the uniqueness results from \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes,lem:exun_frozen_pde}. Since the Banach fixed-point theorem tells us that fixed points are unique, we know that weak solutions to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init} are too.
\end{proof}
