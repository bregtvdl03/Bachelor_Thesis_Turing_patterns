Our goal in this section is to prove the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}{Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions}{eq_and_un}
    Let $u_0, u_{b, 0} \in L^2(\partial \Omega)$ and $v_0 \in L^2(\Omega)$ be non-negative initial conditions. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ and $v \in \pdespace$ to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init}. Moreover, these solutions are also non-negative.
\end{theorem}

As previously mentioned, the proof will be structured around an application of the Banach fixed-point theorem. If you have followed a course on ODE theory, chances are that you have seen the Picard-Lindel\"of Theorem and its proof. The proof for our model follows the same general idea, but requires some extra steps to handle our situation of a coupled system. We ``freeze'' the terms in the ODEs that depend on $v$ so we can use the standard existence and uniqueness properties we know about such systems to get solutions $u$ and $u_b$ to this alternative problem. The PDE gets a similar treatment, producing solutions $v$ of some alternative PDE, after which we can ``update'' the frozen terms in the ODE. This produces an operator $\mathcal{T}$ that maps any $v \in \pdespace$ to another $\widetilde{v} \in \pdespace$. If we can then show that this operator is a contraction, the Banach fixed-point theorem will guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a fixed point $v^* \in \pdespace$, which will be our solution to the PDE.

Let us first concretely demonstrate what we mean by ``freezing'' the terms that depend on $v$. Fix $v^* \in \pdespace$ and consider the modified system of ODEs given by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen_odes}
    \begin{aligned}
        u_t         &= - f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*) + p(u)    & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma, \\
        u_{b,t}     &= f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*) + q(u_b)    & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma. \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}{Existence and uniqueness for the decoupled ODEs}{exun_frozen_odes}
    Let $u_0, u_{b,0} \in L^2(\partial \Omega)$ be non-negative initial conditions and fix $v^* \in \pdespace$. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ to \cref{eq:frozen_odes} satisfying these initial conditions. Moreover, these solutions are also non-negative and bounded by a constant depending on $T$.
\end{lemma}

    \begin{equation*}
        w(t) := \begin{pmatrix}
            u(t, x) \\
            u_b(t, x)
        \end{pmatrix}.
    \end{equation*}
    
    \noindent
    Then \cref{eq:frozen_odes} can be described by

    \begin{equation}\label{eq:cara_ode}
        w'(t) = F(w(t), t),
    \end{equation}

    where $F: \R^2 \times \R \to \R^2$ is defined by

    \begin{equation*}
        F(w, t) := \begin{pmatrix}
            -f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*(t, x)) + p(u) \\
            f(u, u_b, \gamma v^*(t, x)) + q(u_b) \\

    \noindent
    for each $w = (u, u_b) \in \R^2$ and $t \in \R$. Note that this definition only makes sense because both $x$ and $v^*$ are fixed by assumption.

Using \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes}, we define an operator $\mathcal{T}_1: \pdespace \to \odespace^2$ that maps any function $v^* \in \pdespace$ to a tuple $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$ of solutions to \cref{eq:frozen_odes}.

We can use a similar method for the PDE in \cref{sys:PDEs}. Fix $(u^*, u_b^*) \in \odespace^2$ and consider the modified weak formulation given by

This result allows us to define another operator $\mathcal{T}_2: \odespace^2 \to \pdespace$ sending a tuple $(u^*, u_b^*) \in \odespace^2$ to a solution $v \in \pdespace$ of \cref{eq:frozen_pde}. By combining this step with the previous one, we can use $\mathcal{T} := \mathcal{T}_2 \circ \mathcal{T}_1: \pdespace \to \pdespace$ to iteratively get new solutions. At each step, we modify \cref{eq:frozen_odes} using the input from $\pdespace$ to get a new pair $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$, with which we proceed to update \cref{eq:frozen_pde} to get a new $v \in \pdespace$.

\begin{lemma}{}{contraction}
    For $\widetilde{T} > 0$ small enough, $\mathcal{T}: \pdespace \to \pdespace$ is a contraction. That is, there is a $q \in [0, 1)$ such that
    
    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{\mathcal{T} v}_\pdespace \leq q \norm{v}_\pdespace
    \end{equation*}

    for every $v \in \pdespace$.
\end{lemma}

We have now gathered enough results to prove the main statement of this section.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:eq_and_un}]
    Using \cref{lem:contraction}, let $T > 0$ such that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction. Then by the Banach fixed-point theorem, there exists a unique fixed point $v^*$ of $\mathcal{T}$. Next, define $(u^*, u_b^*) := \mathcal{T}_1 v^*$. Then by definition, $u^*$ and $u_b^*$ solve \cref{eq:frozen_odes} with $v^*$ as the frozen input. On the other hand, $\mathcal{T}_2 (u^*, u_b^*) = \mathcal{T}v^* = v^*$ solves \cref{eq:frozen_pde} with $u^*$ and $u_b^*$ as the inputs to the frozen reactions. In other words, $u^*$, $u_b^*$ and $v^*$ are weak solutions to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init}.

    To show uniqueness of the weak solutions, it is enough to note that for any weak solution $u^*, u_b^*$ and $v^*$, we have that $v^*$ is a fixed point of $\mathcal{T}$. This follows directly from the uniqueness results from \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes,lem:exun_frozen_pde}. Since the Banach fixed-point theorem tells us that fixed points are unique, we know that weak solutions to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init} are too.
\end{proof}
