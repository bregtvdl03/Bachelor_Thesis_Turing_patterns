Our goal in this section is to prove the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}{Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions}{eq_and_un}
    Let the initial conditions $u_0, u_{b, 0} \in \Ltwo{\partial \Omega}$ and $v_0 \in \Ltwo{\Omega}$ be essentially bounded and non-negative. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ and $v \in \pdespace$ to \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init}. Moreover, these solutions are also non-negative.
\end{theorem}

As previously mentioned, the proof will be based on an application of Banach's fixed-point theorem. The proof for our model follows the same general idea as the proof of the Picard-Lindel\"of theorem proving the existence and uniqueness for ODEs, but we need to adjust the arguments to handle our situation of a coupled bulk-surface system. The idea is to ``freeze'' the function $v$ in the ODEs so we can use the standard existence and uniqueness theory for ODEs to get solutions $u$ and $u_b$ to this problem. The PDE we treat similarly, freezing the functions $u$ and $u_b$. Having a solution of this PDE, we can ``update'' the frozen terms in the ODE. This produces an operator $\mathcal{T}$ that maps any $v \in \pdespace$ to another $\widetilde{v} \in \pdespace$. If we can then show that this operator is a contraction, Banach's fixed-point theorem will guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a fixed point $v^* \in \pdespace$, which will be our solution to the PDE.

Let us first make precise what we mean by ``freezing'' the function $v$ in the ODE system. Fix $v^* \in \pdespace$ and consider the modified system of ODEs given by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen_odes}
    \begin{aligned}
        u_t         &= P_u + \alpha u_b - \delta_u u - 2 \kon u^2 v^* + 2 \koff u_b   & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma, \\
        u_{b,t}     &= - \delta_{u_b} u_b + \kon u^2 v^* - \koff u_b                  & \text{on }& (0, T) \times \Gamma. \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}{Existence and uniqueness for the decoupled ODEs}{exun_frozen_odes}
    Let the initial conditions $u_0, u_{b,0} \in \Ltwo{\Gamma}$ be essentially bounded and non-negative and fix $v^* \in \pdespace$. Then there exist unique weak solutions $u, u_b \in \odespace$ to \cref{eq:frozen_odes} corresponding to these initial conditions. Moreover, these solutions are also non-negative and bounded by a constant depending on $T$.
\end{lemma}

Using \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes,lem:exun_frozen_pde}, we can define two operators between $\pdespace$ and $\odespace^2$. One takes two arbitrary functions $(u^*, u_b^*) \in \odespace^2$ and maps them to the unique solution $v \in \pdespace$ to \cref{eq:frozen_pde}. Let us refer to this operator as $\mathcal{T}_{1}: \odespace^2 \to \pdespace$. The other operator, $\mathcal{T}_{2}: \pdespace \to \odespace^2$, takes a function $v^* \in \pdespace$ and defines $\mathcal{T}_{2}(v^*)$ as the unique solution $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$ of \cref{eq:frozen_odes}. Combining these two operators, we define

\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{T} := \mathcal{T}_{2} \circ \mathcal{T}_{1} : \odespace^2 \to \odespace^2.
\end{equation*}

\noindent
We want to show that this operator has a unique fixed point. If this is the case, we have functions $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$ such that $\mathcal{T}(u, u_b) = (u, u_b)$, and let $v \in \pdespace$ denote $v = \mathcal{T}_{1}(u, u_b)$. Given that then $\mathcal{T}_{2}(v) = (u, u_b)$, by definition this means that $v$ solves \cref{eq:weak_pde} for almost every $t \in (0, T)$, and $w = (u, u_b)$ solves \cref{eq:weak_ode} for almost every $t \in (0, T)$ and $x \in \Gamma$, meaning that $v$, $u$ and $u_b$ are weak solutions of our model. Conversely, if $v$, $u$ and $u_b$ are weak solutions to the model, then by the uniqueness of solutions in \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes,lem:exun_frozen_pde}, we must have that $\mathcal{T}_{2}(v) = (u, u_b)$ and $\mathcal{T}_{1}(u, u_b) = v$, and thus $\mathcal{T}(u, u_b) = (u, u_b)$.

It should be clear now how we will apply Banach's fixed-point theorem. If we prove that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction, we can guarantee the existence of a fixed point and therefore a weak solution. Since every weak solution of the model is also a fixed point, the theorem also grants us uniqueness of such solutions. To prove that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction, we first show this for $\mathcal{T}_{2}$ and $\mathcal{T}_{1}$ separately, after which we can easily combine the results.

        &\leq B_\eps(T) \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + \koff A_{\eps} \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{\Gamma}}^2 + C_\gamma^2 C(T) \eps \norm{v}_{H^1(\Omega)}^2\\
    \end{align*}
    \endgroup

    \noindent
    for almost all $t$. Now let $\delta := \min (\delta_v, D_v)$ and integrate from 0 to $T$ to get

    \begin{equation*}
        \frac{1}{2} \norm{v(t)}_{\Ltwo{\Omega}}^2 + (\delta - C_\gamma^2 C(T) \eps) \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}}^2 \leq B_\eps(T) \norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \koff A_{\eps} \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    for almost all $t$. If we now choose $\eps < \frac{\delta}{C_\gamma^2 C(T)}$, we can estimate both the $\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}$-norm and the $\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Omega}}$-norm of $v$. The first will only be helpful later, but the latter concludes this proof. First, since $\delta - C_\gamma^2 C(T) \eps > 0$, we have

    \begin{equation}\label{eq:extra_estimate}
    \begin{aligned}
        \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; H^1(\Omega)}}^2 \leq D_\eps(T) \left(\norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2\right) .
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}

    \noindent
    Second, we can integrate from 0 to $T$ once more to get

    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{v}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Omega}}}^2 \leq T M_\eps(T) \left(\norm{u}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2 + \norm{u_b}_{\Ltwo{0, T; \Ltwo{\Gamma}}}^2\right).
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    If we trace back the definition of this constant $M_\eps(T)$ in the proof of \cref{lem:exun_frozen_odes}, we see that it is continuous in $T$ with a finite value at $T = 0$. This means that for small enough $T$, we will have that $T M_\eps(T) < 1$, finishing this proof. \todo{We don't know $M_v(T)$}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}{}{contraction_odes}
    For $T > 0$ small enough, $\mathcal{T}_{2}: \pdespace \to \odespace^2$ is a contraction. That is, there is a $q \in [0, 1)$ such that
    
    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{\mathcal{T}_{2} (\vb) - \mathcal{T}_{2} (\vt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 \leq q \norm{\vb - \vt}_\pdespace^2
    \end{equation*}

    for every $\vb, \vt \in \pdespace$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:eq_and_un}]
    Let $\widetilde{T} > 0$ be small enough such that, by \cref{lem:contraction_pde,lem:contraction_odes}, $\mathcal{T}_1$ and $\mathcal{T}_2$ are contractions with Lipschitz constants $q_1$ and $q_2$, respectively. Define, as discussed before, $\mathcal{T}$ as the composition of $\mathcal{T}_1$ and $\mathcal{T}_2$. Then for any $(\ub, \ubb), (\ut, \ubt) \in \odespace^2$, we have

    \begin{equation*}
        \norm{\mathcal{T}(\ub, \ubb) - \mathcal{T}(\ut, \ubt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 \leq q_2 \norm{\mathcal{T}_1(\ub, \ubb) - \mathcal{T}_1(\ut, \ubt)}_{\pdespace}^2 \leq q_2 q_1 \norm{(\ub, \ubb) - (\ut, \ubt)}_{\odespace^2}^2 ,
    \end{equation*}

    \noindent
    showing that $\mathcal{T}$ is a contraction since $q_2 q_1 \in [0, 1)$.

    We apply Banach's fixed-point theorem to get a unique fixed point $(u, u_b) \in \odespace^2$. As previously discussed, this corresponds to a unique weak solution on $[0, \widetilde{T}]$. By setting new initial conditions $u_0 = u(T)$, $u_{b,0} = u_b(T)$ and $v_0 = v(T) := \mathcal{T}_1(u, u_b)(T)$ and applying the same process, this solution can be extended to $[0, \overline{T}]$ for some $\overline{T} > \widetilde{T}$. We continue repeating these steps until a maximum interval $I_{\max}$ is reached, at which point we distinguish three cases.

    First of all, if $[0, T] \subseteq I_{\max}$, we are immediately done, as we now have a solution on $[0, T]$ with the required properties. If $I_{\max} = [0, T_{\max}]$ for some $T_{\max} < T$, we can set new initial conditions at $u(T_{\max})$, $u_b(T_{\max})$ and $v(T_{\max})$ and extend again, contradicting the fact that $I_{\max}$ is the maximum interval of existence. Lastly, if instead $I_{\max} = [0, T_{\max})$ for some $T_{\max} < T$, we apply \cref{thm:lionsmagenes} to extend $v$ to a continuous function on $[0, T_{\max}]$. This allows us to use the same method again and extend the solution.

\end{proof}
