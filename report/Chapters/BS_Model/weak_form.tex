Before we start proving the existence of solutions to this model, we must discuss what kind of solutions we are looking for. Specifically, we will derive the weak formulations of \cref{sys:ODEs,sys:PDEs,sys:Init} and discuss the function spaces that are involved.

As in \cref{sec:weak_forms}, we first suppose that $u$, $u_b$ and $v$ are smooth solutions. Then we can interpret them as functions $\mathbf{u}: [0, T] \to L^2(\Gamma)$, $\mathbf{u_b}: [0, T] \to L^2(\Gamma)$ and $\mathbf{v}: [0, T] \to H^1(\Omega)$, defined by

\begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{u}(t) := u(t, \cdot),   \quad\quad  \mathbf{u_b}(t) := u_b(t, \cdot)    \quad\quad  \text{and } \quad\quad  \mathbf{v}(t) := v(t, \cdot).
\end{equation*}

\noindent
Now if we denote the weak time derivatives of $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{u_b}$ by $\mathbf{u}'$ and $\mathbf{u_b}'$, respectively, we can write \cref{sys:ODEs} as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak_ode}
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{u}'     &= -f(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{u_b}, \mathbf{v}) + p(\mathbf{u}), \\
        \mathbf{u_b}'   &= f(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{u_b}, \mathbf{v}) + q(\mathbf{u_b}).
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent
Weak solutions of \cref{sys:ODEs} are then required to satisfy \cref{eq:weak_ode} for almost every $t \in [0, T]$ and $x \in \Omega$.

Contrary to the ODEs, the PDE is not defined separately for every $x$. Instead, we have to work on the entire domain simultaneously. By multiplying $\mathbf{v}'$ by a test function $\phi \in C^\infty_c(\Omega)$ and integrating on $\Omega$ for fixed $t$, we get

\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \int_\Omega \mathbf{v}' \phi = D \int_\Omega \Delta \mathbf{v} \phi     &= - D \int_\Omega \nabla \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla \phi + D \int_{\partial \Omega} \pderiv[\mathbf{v}]{\nu} \phi \ dS \\
                                                                                &= - D \int_\Omega \nabla \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla \phi + D \int_{\Gamma} \pderiv[\mathbf{v}]{\nu} \phi \ dS                 + D \int_{\Gamma_e} \pderiv[\mathbf{v}]{\nu} \phi \ dS \\
                                                                                &= - D \int_\Omega \nabla \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla \phi - \int_{\Gamma} f(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{u_b}, \mathbf{v}) \phi \ dS    + \int_{\Gamma} r(\mathbf{v}) \phi \ dS, \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

\noindent
which we can also write as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak_pde}
    \inner{\mathbf{v}', \phi}_{L^2(\Omega)} = - D \inner{\nabla \mathbf{v}, \nabla \phi}_{L^2(\Omega; \R^d)} - \inner{f(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{u_b}, \mathbf{v}), \phi}_{L^2(\Gamma)} + \inner{r(\mathbf{v}), \phi}_{L^2(\Gamma)}.
\end{equation}

Since we have used integration with respect to space in this computation, this expression only depends on time. A weak solution of \cref{sys:PDEs} is therefore required to satisfy \cref{eq:weak_pde} at almost every $t \in [0, T]$.
